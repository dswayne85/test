## Week of July 28 – August 3

### 1. Client marketing meeting + immediate LSA account audit (Jul 28)
Held the scheduled McCranie Marketing Meeting via Zoom with Danielle and the firm's team. Immediately after the meeting, we pulled up the firm's Local Services Ads (LSA) account and found that the **traffic-ticket practice-area settings were still enabled** on the profile. We corrected the settings and sent Danielle a written recap the same day ("LSAs and Reports").
**Why it matters (conversions):** LSA budget was being spent attracting low-value traffic-ticket calls instead of DUI/criminal-defense leads. Correcting the practice-area targeting redirects ad spend toward the case types the firm actually wants, improving lead quality and cost-per-signed-case.

### 2. Logged and assigned the firm's Web Production Request (Jul 28)
Received Javier's Web Production Request document, distributed it to the web team, and put it under active follow-up tracking so every item would be worked and reported on at the next Tuesday review.
**Why it matters (conversions/SEO):** The request contained on-site changes (reviews section, landing page, content edits) that directly affect user experience and conversion paths.

### 3. Google support investigation — pinpointed the LSA change date (Jul 29–30)
Alfredo asked when the LSA/GMB settings had been modified. We contacted Google support and got confirmation that **the LSA settings change was made on July 16**. We pushed further and asked Google to identify which user account made the change; Google confirmed their logs capture only the date, not the user.
**Why it matters (conversions):** Establishing the exact date of the misconfiguration lets us correlate the lead-volume dip against a known cause, defend the recovery timeline, and prove the issue was identified and fixed.

### 4. Updating.AI vendor evaluation and AI-search guidance (Jul 31)
The firm asked us to evaluate the Updating.AI platform for improving their AI search results. We advised that Updating.AI **only promises that the site will be indexed by AI services — it does not guarantee the firm will be mentioned or recommended** in AI answers, and that indexing alone is table stakes. We followed up by sending the firm our AI Search Optimization guide describing what actually drives AI-assistant citations.
**Why it matters (AI SEO):** Prevented the client from spending on a tool that wouldn't move AI visibility, and refocused strategy on the factors that do: entity consistency, review signals, structured content, and authoritative citations.

### 5. Reviews-section strategy for the website (Jul 31)
As part of the web request, we proposed adding a "Leave a Review" button linking directly to the firm's Google Business Profile and clarified the firm's preference (reviews solicited only after a first point of contact with a prospect).
**Why it matters (SEO + conversions):** Review velocity and rating are among the strongest local-pack ranking factors and the top trust signal prospects check before calling.

### 6. Citation audit and building for all three offices (Jul 31 – Aug 1)
Updated the citation workbooks for **Valdosta, Tifton, and Douglas** and delivered the weekly citations report covering July 27–31.
**Why it matters (SEO + AI SEO):** Consistent name/address/phone data across directories is foundational for local rankings — and AI assistants (ChatGPT, Gemini, AI Overviews) lean heavily on consistent entity data when deciding which businesses to recommend.

### 12. Sitewide blog upgrade — author bios, schema, internal links, citations on every post (Aug 1–4)
Executed a full E-E-A-T and structured-data upgrade across the **entire blog archive (~210 posts)** via the WordPress REST API. A small 5-post batch ran Aug 1, a 3-post pilot validated the template on Aug 3, and the full rollout ran Aug 4 (posts updated roughly every 12 seconds over about an hour). Each post received:

- **"About the author" bio block** — George F. McCranie IV, Founder; admitted to the Georgia State Bar 1996; former Assistant District Attorney, Alapaha Judicial Circuit; member of the National College for DUI Defense; fellow of the Lawyers Foundation of Georgia; former State Bar of Georgia Board of Governors member.
- **"Published / Last reviewed" dateline** with machine-readable `<time>` markup (e.g., "Published 2018-06-06 · Last reviewed August 3, 2026").
- **Article JSON-LD schema** embedded per post: headline, URL, `mainEntityOfPage`, `datePublished`, `dateModified`, `lastReviewed`, `author` as a `Person` (linked to the attorney's about page, `worksFor` the `LegalService` "McCranie Law Firm"), `publisher` `Organization` with logo, `inLanguage`, and `isPartOf` the site's `WebSite` entity.
- **"Related Georgia Legal Resources" internal-links module** pointing to the firm's criminal-defense money pages.
- **Authoritative outbound citations** to official legal sources (e.g., O.C.G.A. Title 16 on Justia).

**Outcome:** Verified live on the site — bio, dateline, schema, internal links, and citations all render on the published posts, and the WordPress API confirms the archive-wide modification run on Aug 4.
**Why it matters (SEO + AI SEO + conversions):** Google's quality systems and AI assistants both weigh E-E-A-T — *who* wrote the content and what their credentials are. The author bio and Person/LegalService schema tie every article to a named, credentialed attorney entity; `lastReviewed` datelines signal content freshness; internal links funnel the archive's authority into the pages that convert; and citations to primary legal sources are exactly what AI models look for when deciding which content to trust and quote. This work directly supports the 84% ChatGPT Share of AI Voice measured the same day (item 11).
